Question 1: Hypothesis Formation - Why might high altitude affect baseball performance? Is Coors Field affected by high altitude?

Answer:

Why might high altitude affect baseball performance?

At higher altitudes, the air is thinner, meaning there is less air resistance (drag) on a baseball when it is hit or thrown. This allows the ball to travel farther and faster, leading to more home runs and higher scoring games. Additionally, pitchers might find their breaking balls less effective because the reduced air pressure affects the ball’s movement. Also, players might experience changes in stamina and breathing due to the lower oxygen levels, which can impact overall performance.

Is Coors Field affected by high altitude?

Yes, Coors Field in Denver, Colorado, is famously affected by its high altitude—about 5,200 feet above sea level. This elevation makes it one of the most hitter-friendly ballparks in Major League Baseball because the thin air allows balls to carry farther. This results in more runs scored and exciting offensive displays during games played there, distinguishing Coors Field from other stadiums at lower altitudes.

Important🤔 Discussion Questions: Stage 3 - Structuring of Visual Mappings

Question 1: Geometry Choices - Why is a horizontal bar chart better than a scatter plot for this data?

Answer:

A horizontal bar chart is better than a scatter plot in this case because:

  • Categorical axis clarity: The stadium names (e.g., “COL”, “BOS”, “LAD”) are categorical variables, not continuous numbers. Bar charts are designed for discrete categories, while scatter plots work best for numeric or continuous data.

Ease of comparison: In a horizontal bar chart, it’s easy to compare the lengths of bars side by side. This helps us immediately see which stadiums have higher average runs.

  • Label readability: Horizontal layouts make text labels (stadium abbreviations) easier to read, especially when there are many categories. Scatter plots with categorical axes tend to look cluttered and labels can overlap.

  • Perceptual accuracy: Human vision compares lengths along a common scale more accurately than comparing positions of scattered points. This improves data comprehension.

  • When would you choose a vertical bar chart over horizontal?

Answer:

A vertical bar chart is preferable under several conditions:

-Fewer categories: When the dataset includes a limited number of categories, x-axis labels remain legible in a vertical format.

  • Ordered or temporal data: When the data represent time periods or sequences (e.g., months, seasons), a left-to-right layout reflects the natural progression of time.

  • Audience expectations: Viewers often associate temporal or ranked data with a horizontal x-axis progression.

  • Spatial considerations: Vertical bar charts may be more suitable when the available visual space is constrained vertically.

Question 2: Aesthetic Mappings - What does the color highlighting accomplish in Approach 4?

Answer:

The use of color highlighting—assigning “darkorchid” to Colorado and “lightgrey” to other stadiums—serves as a visual emphasis tool that directs the viewer’s attention toward the focal point of the analysis.

In this visualization, the narrative centers on Coors Field (COL) and its reputation as a “run-friendly” ballpark. The application of distinct coloring:

  • Draws immediate attention to the Colorado bar.

  • Reinforces the analytical narrative by distinguishing the key observation from contextual data.

  • Enhances contrast and cognitive focus for the intended audience.

  • How does position (x/y) compare to color for encoding data?

Answer:

Position (x/y) serves as the most precise and effective encoding channel for quantitative data, while color functions primarily as a tool for emphasis or categorization.

  • Position: Enables accurate quantitative comparison through consistent spatial alignment along a common scale.

  • Color: Conveys categorical distinctions or highlights specific data points but lacks precision for representing exact values.

# Stage 4: Formatting for Your Audience
# Create a professional, publication-ready visualization

# Set style for professional appearance
plt.style.use("seaborn-v0_8-whitegrid")

# Create the main visualization
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 6))

# Create color array for highlighting Colorado
colorado_colors = ["darkorchid" if stadium == "COL" else "lightgrey" 
                   for stadium in avgDF_2010_sorted.home]

# Create horizontal bar chart
bars = ax.barh(avgDF_2010_sorted.home, avgDF_2010_sorted.totalRuns, color=colorado_colors)

# Add title and labels
ax.set_title("Colorado (COL) is the Most Run-Friendly Ballpark in 2010", 
             fontsize=16, fontweight='bold', pad=20)
ax.set_xlabel("Average Runs Per Game", fontsize=12)
ax.set_ylabel("Stadium (Home Team)", fontsize=12)

# Add legend
colorado_bar = plt.Rectangle((0,0),1,1, color="darkorchid", label="Colorado Rockies")
other_bar = plt.Rectangle((0,0),1,1, color="lightgrey", label="Other Stadiums")
ax.legend(handles=[colorado_bar, other_bar], loc='lower right', frameon=True)

# Add annotation for Colorado
colorado_index = avgDF_2010_sorted[avgDF_2010_sorted.home == "COL"].index[0]
colorado_runs = avgDF_2010_sorted[avgDF_2010_sorted.home == "COL"]["totalRuns"].iloc[0]
ax.annotate(f"COL: {colorado_runs:.2f} runs/game", 
            xy=(colorado_runs, colorado_index), 
            xytext=(colorado_runs + 0.5, colorado_index),
            arrowprops=dict(arrowstyle='->', color='darkorchid', lw=2),
            fontsize=10, fontweight='bold', color='darkorchid')

# Set x-axis to start from 0 for better comparison
ax.set_xlim(0, max(avgDF_2010_sorted.totalRuns) * 1.1)

# Add grid for easier reading
ax.grid(True, alpha=0.3)

plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

# Print summary statistics
print(f"\nSummary Statistics for 2010:")
print(f"Colorado (COL) average runs per game: {colorado_runs:.2f}")
print(f"League average runs per game: {avgDF_2010_sorted.totalRuns.mean():.2f}")
print(f"Colorado is {((colorado_runs / avgDF_2010_sorted.totalRuns.mean()) - 1) * 100:.1f}% above league average")
